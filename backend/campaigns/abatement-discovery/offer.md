# Offer — there is no offer yet, and the messages must not pretend otherwise

This campaign sells nothing. No product exists, no beta exists, no waitlist exists. The goal
is ten honest conversations about how abatement contractors actually get their state
notifications filed, so we can find out whether this is a real business before building it.

**Never imply a product exists.** Do not write "we built", "our platform", "our tool", "early
access", "beta", or "waitlist". If a draft would embarrass the operator when a contractor
replies "sure, show me the software", the draft is wrong.

**What Chance can truthfully say:** he builds software for small businesses, he is looking at
whether the state notification filing is worth automating, and he wants to know how they
handle it today. That is the whole story and it is enough.

## What they get

An honest answer and nothing else on the first touch. No lead magnet, no report, no promise of
a discount later. If someone spends fifteen minutes explaining their filing process, Chance
offers to send back what other contractors said about the same thing once he has talked to
enough of them. He only offers that because he intends to do it, and he never claims to have
heard something he has not heard yet.

## What we are trying to learn

The point of every conversation is what they DID, not what they think of an idea. Contractors
will happily tell you a filing tool sounds useful; that answer is worthless. These are worth
something:

- Walk me through the last notification you filed. What did you have open in front of you?
- Who actually does it, and what else is that person responsible for?
- How often does a job move after you have filed, and what happens then?
- Has a notification ever come back deficient? What did that cost you?
- Have you ever paid anyone outside the company to handle any of this?

The last question matters most. Someone already paying a consultant or an admin to do this
has proven the budget exists. Everyone else is telling you a story about a budget.

## The disqualifier we most want to find

If contractors say the portals are fine, filing takes ten minutes, and revisions are rare,
this idea is dead and the round ends. That is a successful outcome reached cheaply. Draft the
messages so that answer is easy and comfortable to give — a defensive question that pushes
people toward agreeing there is a problem produces exactly the false positive that has already
cost six rounds of research.
