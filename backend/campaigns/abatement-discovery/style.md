# Style — abatement discovery

Follow the operator's standard first-touch shape. Greeting and self intro, what he does in
everyday words, why he is writing, ONE simple question, reply or phone, thanks.

Model to match:

> Hi, I'm Chance and I build software for small contractors. I'm trying to find out if the
> state notification filing is worth automating or if it's fine as is. Who files your 10 day
> notices, and about how long does one take? Happy to hear it either way, or 3237101190.
> Thanks

## Hard rules

- **No dashes at all.** No hyphens, no en dashes, no em dashes anywhere in message text.
- Short plain sentences. Contractions. Vary the opener across drafts.
- Four short sentences, roughly 35 to 50 words. Connect notes stay under 285 characters.
- Phone number 3237101190 in every first touch, as reply-or-call, never as a demand.
- Never open with an industry observation. Nobody starts a message that way.
- No "not a pitch", no "quick question", no flattery, no meeting ask on a first touch.
- No unnamed social proof. Never claim other contractors said something until they have.

## Words to avoid

Say it the way a contractor would say it out loud. Banned: streamline, leverage, solution,
compliance burden, pain point, workflow, optimize, seamless, robust, reach out, circle back,
touch base, synergy, best in class, cutting edge.

Say "filing", "the 10 day notice", "the portal", "the state", "paperwork". Those are the words
they use.

## The closing question

This is the only thing that varies between A/B arms. Every arm keeps the same greeting and
intro. Each must be answerable in one line by reply.

- **a (who):** "Who files your 10 day notices?"
- **b (time):** "About how long does one notification take you?"
- **c (revisions):** "When a job moves, is refiling a hassle or no big deal?"
- **d (open):** "Is the filing annoying enough to be worth fixing, or is it fine?"

Arm d is deliberately easy to say no to. That is the point. A question people can only agree
with produces a false positive, and this campaign exists to find the truth, not encouragement.
